You are role-playing as a human user about to ask an AI assistant for help with a problem. Your goal is to generate a realistic **initial query** that reflects your current knowledge state.

## ⚠ HARD CONSTRAINTS (override everything below)

These constraints take ABSOLUTE PRIORITY. Apply them BEFORE any other rule.

### HC-1. CANNOT-USE rule for unaware concepts

For any concept in `unaware_concepts`, your `message` MUST NOT:
- Propose any specific value, formula, expression, or application that *uses* that concept
- Reference it by its specific mechanics (e.g., for an unaware "binomial coefficient" concept, you may NOT write "6 choose 2 = 15" or "C(4,2)" or "6!/(2!4!)")
- Recall or reproduce its definition

You may *recognize* the concept exists (acknowledge that some method might be needed) but must NOT *apply* it. Use plain-language confusion: "I don't know how to count this", "I haven't learned the formula for X", "Is there a method for this kind of problem?"

### HC-2. ATTEMPT-WITH-VISIBLE-ERROR rule for struggling concepts

If you reference a concept in `struggling_concepts`, your `message` MUST contain at least ONE of:
- A visible mistake (wrong formula, mixed-up definition, swapped operands, off-by-one)
- Direct uncertainty marker ("I think... but I'm not sure", "I might be mixing this up")
- A request for re-explanation ("Can you explain X again?")

Do NOT produce a clean, confident, correct attempt for a struggling concept.

### HC-3. Required confusion lexicon

If your message references any concept in `unaware_concepts` or `struggling_concepts`, the message MUST contain at least one explicit confusion marker:

> "I don't know" · "I haven't learned" · "I'm not sure what X means" · "I think X means... but I'm not confident" · "I might be confusing X with..." · "I don't fully get" · "I'm a bit stuck on" · "Can you explain X again" · "I haven't seen" · "I don't recognize"

### HC-4. Plain-language fallback

When in doubt, prefer plain-language confusion over symbolic guessing. A real novice doesn't fabricate "C(4,2)=6" if they've never learned what `C` means — they say "I don't know how to count this" or "is there some shortcut for this?".
